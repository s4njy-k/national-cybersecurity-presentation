National Cybersecurity Strategy

A Guide to Developing Resilience

Based on the 3rd Edition (2025) framework for national leaders and policymakers to build secure, resilient, digitally empowered societies.

Core Objective: Protect the availability, integrity, and confidentiality of assets in connected infrastructures of government, private organizations, and citizens.

Why a National Strategy?

Aligning Security with Economic Vision

  • Economic & Social Prosperity: Digitalization drives growth, but only if infrastructure is secure and trusted.
  • Fundamental Human Rights: Rights offline must be protected online (freedom of expression, privacy, data protection).
  • Strategic Alignment: Moves beyond reactive measures to a holistic view across the national digital ecosystem.

The NCS Lifecycle

A Continuous Process of Improvement

Cybersecurity is an ongoing effort, not a one-time project.

  1. Initiation: Identify lead authority, governance, and resource plans.
  2. Stocktaking & Analysis: Assess current cyber-risk landscape.
  3. Sustainable Funding: Secure multi-year resource planning.
  4. Production: Draft and formally approve the Strategy.
  5. Implementation: Execute action plans with clear metrics (SMART APIs).
  6. Monitoring & Evaluation: Measure outcomes and review periodically.

Overarching Principles

Guiding Tenets for the Strategy

  • 1. Vision: Clear whole-of-society goal.
  • 2. Comprehensive Approach: Tailored priorities.
  • 3. Inclusiveness: Active participation.
  • 4. Prosperity: Foster economic growth.
  • 5. Human Rights: Respect offline rights online.
  • 6. Risk & Resilience: Manage, don't eliminate.
  • 7. Policy Instruments: Mix of laws, incentives.
  • 8. Leadership: Clear roles & resources.
  • 9. Trust: Build a trusted digital environment.
  • 10. Adaptability: Technological foresight.

Focus Areas 1 & 2

Governance & Critical Infrastructure

Governance: Designate a competent national authority anchored at the highest level of government. Ensure intra-government and sectoral coordination.
Critical Infrastructure (CI/CII/ES): Implement formal approaches to identify operators. Define targeted cybersecurity baseline requirements rather than rigid technical prescriptions.
5 / 7

Focus Areas 3 & 4

Risk Management & Incident Response

  • National Risk Framework: Establish a dynamic national risk register based on a common methodology. Adopt regular assessment cycles.
  • Incident Response: Establish National and Sectoral CERTs/CSIRTs. Create crisis contingency plans.
  • Collaboration: Promote threat intelligence sharing (ISACs) and conduct regular national & international cybersecurity exercises to test readiness.
6 / 7

Focus Areas 5, 6, & 7

Capacity, Legislation, & International Cooperation

  • Capacity Building: Develop formal cybersecurity education pathways and coordinate national awareness campaigns.
  • Legislation: Establish robust domestic legal frameworks for cybersecurity and cybercrime (e.g., electronic evidence, data protection).
  • International Cooperation: Embed cyber within foreign policy, engage in international norms (UN, Budapest Convention), and pursue cross-border legal harmonization.
